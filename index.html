Bayda — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 4 September 2026

Bayda is a training, running and nutrition tracker for iOS. This policy describes exactly what

it stores, where, and for how long. It is written against the app's actual database schema

rather than from a template — every category below corresponds to a real table.

Controller: David Bayard (Baydalabs), Belgium. Contact: bayard.david@hotmail.com

What Bayda stores

Health data (special category)

Bayda stores weight, body fat percentage, food and drink you log, and your training —

exercises, sets, weights, repetitions, durations and session times.

Under the GDPR this is special-category data concerning health (Article 9). The lawful

basis is your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a)), given by creating an account and entering

it. You can withdraw that consent at any time by deleting your account, which erases the data

(see Deleting your account).

This data is never shared with anyone. There is no setting that shares it. It is readable

only by your own account, enforced in the database itself and not merely in the app.

Account and profile

Your account identifier, and the username, display name, biography and profile picture you

choose. Your username is how other people find you — your email address is never searchable

and is never shown to another user.

Training you choose to share

Bayda has a social feed. You can look up another account by username, follow it, and see the workouts of the people you follow; they can see yours on the same terms.

Who can see your workouts is the visibility setting on your profile, and it defaults to followers only. The three values are private (nobody but you), followers only (the accounts that follow you), and any Bayda account. The setting is read at the moment somebody tries to look, not stored onto each workout — so changing it applies to everything you have already logged as well as everything new, in both directions. Tightening it hides your existing workouts immediately.

You can also set a single workout to followers only or only me. That setting can only make one workout more private than your account already is; it can never make it less. On a followers-only account, marking a workout “everyone” does not publish it.

A private account is a separate switch. With it on, nobody can follow you until you accept them: their request is stored, it is visible to you in Profile › Follow requests, and until you accept it that person can see nothing of your training — the request grants no access of any kind. Declining a request deletes it. People already following you when you turn the switch on stay followers.

Follows, likes and comments are stored, because storing them is what makes them work. A like or a comment carries your account and is visible to everyone who can see the workout it is on. Deleting a comment removes it.

Your email address is never searchable and is never shown to another user. Accounts are found by exact username only, and only while you leave Findable by username switched on. A username that does not exist, one that has switched discoverability off, and one that has blocked you all return the same empty result, so the search cannot be used to test whether an account exists.

Tagging someone you trained with

You can tag another Bayda account on a session you recorded — the person you ran or lifted with. Two separate things happen, and the difference between them is deliberate.

On your own card, immediately. The tag is part of your post, so their name appears on it as soon as you add it, to everyone who can already see that workout under the rules above. This needs no permission from them, in the same way that writing a name in a caption does not.

On their profile, only if they accept. They are notified, and until they say yes the session does not appear on their profile and their name is not shown publicly on your card either — only you, in that workout's own screen, can see a tag nobody has answered yet. They can accept, decline, or withdraw an acceptance later. Declining is not announced to you; the tag simply stops appearing.

A tag lets the tagged person read that session, from the moment they are tagged, so they can see what they are being asked about — even if the workout would otherwise be outside what they are allowed to see. Declining takes that access away again. The GPS route is never included: being tagged on a run does not give anyone the route it followed, because a route usually starts and ends at somebody's home.

Only the account owner can tag on their own session, and only the tagged person can answer. Nobody can add a name to a workout that is not theirs, and nobody can answer on someone else's behalf.

Bayda does not compare your location history against other people's to guess who you trained with. Some apps do this. Bayda does not do it, and does not run any matching of that kind in the background. Tagging is something you do by hand, or not at all.

Location, while you record a run or a walk

Bayda records your location only while a run or a walk is recording, and only if you allow it. It asks for “while using the app” permission — never “always” — so it cannot locate you when you are not recording. The background permission it does use keeps a recording you started running with the screen off, and stops when you stop.

What is kept is the path itself: a list of the positions your phone reported, stored with the workout, plus the distance, pace and elevation worked out from it. Positions your phone reported inaccurately are discarded rather than stored, and when the signal drops out Bayda never invents a path across the gap — the route you and anyone else see is only the ground your phone actually reported.

Steps and motion, if you allow it

Bayda can read your step count from your phone's motion sensor, which is a separate permission from location and is optional. It asks the first time you record a walk, or the first time a run loses its GPS signal — not when you sign up.

It is used for two things. It shows how many steps a run or a walk took, and when GPS drops out it asks the step counter how far you went during exactly that stretch, so the distance is not missing the tunnel you ran through. Any distance worked out this way is labelled on the summary as an estimate, with its size in kilometres, and it never changes your route or your pace — both of those stay measured from GPS alone. Steps are read on your phone; the step count itself is not uploaded.

If you refuse this permission, or your phone has no step counter, Bayda behaves exactly as it did before: a stretch with no GPS is reported as not counted, and the steps figure shows a dash rather than a zero.

GPS routes are governed by a separate setting, because a route usually begins and ends at your home. A new route takes the more private of that setting and the run’s own visibility, so if your routes are set to private a public run still posts without a map. The setting is stamped onto each route when it is saved, so loosening it later never retroactively publishes a route you already recorded.

What other people see of a route, and what they do not

When someone else can see one of your routes, they are not shown the route you recorded. Before it is ever published, Bayda’s database cuts a randomly chosen distance — between 150 and 450 metres, drawn separately for each end of each run — off the start and off the finish. The map on a feed card is that shortened line, and it is labelled to say the ends are hidden. Only you can see your own complete route.

This happens on the server, not in the app, and the full route is stored in a table that no other account can read under any setting. We do it this way deliberately: a trim performed by the app would still leave the complete path sitting in data another person’s phone could fetch.

A run short enough to lie entirely inside its own hidden zone publishes no map at all rather than a fragment. The run itself still appears, with its distance and time.

We should be straight with you about the limits of this. Hiding the ends of a route raises the effort needed to work out where you live; it does not make it impossible. Published research on other apps that do the same thing has shown that with enough activities from the same starting point, and with the total distance of each one known, a determined person can narrow down a hidden location. Choosing a random distance each time is what defeats the simplest version of that attack, and it is not a guarantee. If you would rather share nothing, set your route visibility to private — your runs will still post, without maps.

If you turn off Precise Location, Bayda will not record a run at all. The positions it would get are accurate to about a kilometre, which is not a route, and recording one anyway would mean storing a path you did not take.

Apple Health

Each of these is a separate permission you can refuse individually, and Bayda works without any of them.

What it reads. Your step count, to show what you have actually walked instead of guessing. Your weight and body fat percentage, so you do not have to re-type what your scale already recorded. When you tap Fill from Apple Health while setting up a calorie target, your height, date of birth and sex, which every calorie formula needs. After a run, walk or ride, the heart rate your watch recorded during it and the energy it measured, so the summary can show what you actually burned rather than Bayda's estimate of it. And, only if you ask it to, a list of the runs, walks and rides other apps and watches recorded in the last 30 days, so you can choose which of them to bring in. It never imports an activity you have not picked.

What it writes. The workouts, runs, walks and rides you finish, with their distance and an estimate of the energy burned, so they count towards your Activity rings. For an outdoor activity it also writes the route it recorded, so the map appears in Health beside the workout. That copy is the complete route, not the shortened one other people see — it is on your own device, in your own Health store.

What leaves your device, and what does not. Your steps, heart rate, height, date of birth and sex are read and used on your device only — they are never uploaded to Bayda's servers. Two things are different, and both are things you ask for: a weight you choose to save becomes a weigh-in on your account, and a session you pick out of the import list is copied to your account as a workout of your own, with its date, duration, distance and energy. That copy is then treated exactly like a session Bayda recorded itself, including the visibility rules above. Nothing is copied until you tap Add on that particular session.

You can revoke any of it at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health.

Photos on a workout

If you add a photo to a workout or a run, it is uploaded to Bayda's storage and shown to whoever can see that workout — the same set of people its privacy setting already governs, and no one else. A private session's photo is private; a followers-only session's photo is visible to followers.

Before this, a photo you added was stored only on the phone that took it, so nobody else ever saw one. That has changed, and it is worth saying plainly: a photo on a workout is shared content, governed by that workout's visibility. Deleting the workout deletes the photo with it.

Notifications

If you enable them, Bayda sends you reminders you set yourself — to weigh in, to log a meal, to train. These are scheduled on your device and their content never leaves it.

Bayda also stores your device’s push token so that the server can notify you when somebody follows you, or likes or comments on one of your workouts. The notification’s content is generated on the server at the moment it is sent; the request that triggers the send carries only an identifier and a timestamp, never your name and never the text of a comment.

Reports

If you report content, Bayda stores the report, what it referred to, and who sent it, so it can

be reviewed.

What Bayda does NOT do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No third-party analytics or tracking SDKs.
  • Your data is never sold, rented, or shared with advertisers or data brokers.
  • No email/password accounts — sign-in is Apple or Google only, so Bayda never handles a

password.

Where it is stored

Bayda uses Supabase (database, authentication, file storage and server functions) hosted in

the European Union (eu-west-1, Ireland). Supabase acts as a processor.

Push notifications are delivered through Apple Push Notification service. Sign-in is handled

by Apple and, if you choose it, Google; Bayda receives only the identifier and, on first

sign-in with Apple, the name you allow it to share.

How long it is kept

Your data is kept for as long as your account exists. When you delete your account it is erased

immediately — see below. Username-search rate-limit records are discarded after two hours.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you may access, correct, export, restrict, object to, or erase your data. Two of

these are built into the app and need no request:

  • Export — Profile → Your data → Export my data. Produces a complete JSON file of

everything held about you.

  • Erasure — Profile → Your data → Delete my account.

For anything else, or to complain, write to bayard.david@hotmail.com. You also have the right to

lodge a complaint with the Belgian Data Protection Authority (gegevensbeschermingsautoriteit.be).

Deleting your account

Deletion removes your profile, workouts, sets, routes, food log, weight history, recipes,

comments, likes, follows and blocks.

Stored files are deleted too, not just the records pointing at them — your profile picture

and any exercise photos are removed from storage before any row is deleted, and the deletion is

refused if any file survives. Bayda keeps only a one-way hash of the deleted account identifier

and the date, as evidence the erasure was carried out; that hash cannot be used to identify you.

Children

Bayda is not intended for anyone under 16 and should not be used by them.

Changes
